You wake up in an airport. Every flight is on schedule. Everything works, but there's nobody here.

TERMINALS is a first-person casual horror puzzle game. Miles Ashby, 21, wakes up in a car in the flooded parking lot of an empty airport, with no memory of arriving. Kiosks print, belts run, and a voice on the PA calls him by his name.

He does what airports ask: check in, drop the bag, clear security, find the gate. Every task is ordinary and slightly wrong, and every time the lights go out, the terminal has rearranged itself behind him.

Note: there is no combat and no inventory. One thing in the terminal comes after you; if it catches you, you start the area again. The game plays on procedure, empty spaces and the feeling of being watched.

The game

  • The airport is the puzzle. Every airport procedure hides one.
  • It rearranges behind you. The space you just crossed is never the same.
  • A chase, not a fight. No weapons. You walk, look, carry, throw and wait.
  • The Passengers. Shadows that move when you look away.
  • Three endings. A run lasts about 90 minutes. Hidden areas unlock two more endings.
  • Made to be watched. Easy-to-clip scares and simple controls on any platform.

Who you meet

  • Miles Ashby. Flying alone for the first time, to see a band.
  • Ada. The voice of the terminal. She knows his name.
  • Gene. The only other passenger. Drops the hints you need, then vanishes.
  • The King of Pain. The one presence in the terminal that comes after you.
  • The Passengers. Silhouettes that sit, queue and wait, and react to you.

Inspired by

TERMINALS draws on the mythology around Denver International Airport: the underground tunnels, the murals, the blue horse with red eyes, and decades of conspiracy theories the airport itself plays along with.

  • Kane Pixels and The Backrooms (Found Footage)
  • Severance
  • Twin Peaks and David Lynch
  • Crystal Castles, the band, and the look of their music videos
